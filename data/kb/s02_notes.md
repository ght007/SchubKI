# S02 slide transcriptions

S02 (AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf) is mostly images, so PDF text extraction returns little.
These notes are a careful transcription of what each slide shows. Text in quotes is printed on
the slide; text after "Figure:" describes a chart or picture. Each section is ingested as the
text of that slide and is cited as "S02 p.N (transcribed)".

## S02 p.1
Title slide: "Process-Control in-line with Spectral Fiber Systems". Authors: Slava Artyushenko, Boris Mizaikoff, Martin Warman, Brad Swarbrick, Polina Fomina, Alex Novikov.

## S02 p.2
"Spectroscopy Market forecast". "Main drivers of the growth this market: food safety concerns; growth of pharmaceutical & biotechnology industry; technological advancements in molecular spectroscopy & their application in environmental screening."
Figure: global molecular spectroscopy market by application 2020-2027; market shown growing from about 5 to 10 billion USD (2019 to 2027). Business context only.

## S02 p.3
"Choice of optimal System & Method for process-control in-line". "Multi-Spectral Fiber Systems allow to test 4 spectroscopy methods in 0.3-16µm range - using robust fiber probes for remote monitoring of chemical reaction in-line. Comparison of spectral data for customer process enables to select the best process-control solution."
Figure: a modular multispectral fiber system with Raman, FTIR, Fluorescence and NIR modules feeding "Chemometric analysis of data fusion" and "Feedback", connected to a "Multispectral fiber probe of customised structure" in a reactor. Probe types pictured: Diamond ATR, Raman, Transflexion, Reflex, ATR-loop, Fluorescence ("Fiber probes compatible with any spectrometer").

## S02 p.4
"20 years History of Multi-Spectral Fiber System from Martin Warman". Quote on the slide: "use of 'live process spectroscopy' is not new, and it isn't used because of a technology limitation, in many cases it is simply used for development, so when the process is stable it is not needed - as a simple sensor version is adequate in commercial production". "Flow cell with 254nm LED and detector in C276 stainless steel housing used for transmission control changes with time."

## S02 p.5
"Muli-Spectral Fiber Systems to select the Best Process Control Method".
Figure: attenuation curves of UV-Silica and NIR-Silica fibers (wavelength axis up to about 2.75 µm) and of CIR-Fibers and PIR-Fibers (wavelength axis up to about 18 µm); the same modular system diagram as p.3 (Raman, FTIR, Fluorescence, NIR, chemometric data fusion, feedback, customised multispectral probe in a reactor).

## S02 p.6
"Transmission for Different Fibers in 0.2-18µm - used for Fiber Probes".
Figure: transmission ranges of fiber families on a wavelength axis from UV to MIR:
- Standard and Al/Cu-coated UV-VIS silica fibers: 180 nm to 1200 nm.
- Standard and Al/Cu-coated VIS-NIR silica fibers: 350 nm to 2400 nm.
- Chalcogenide As-S IR fibers (CIR): 1100 nm to 6500 nm.
- Polycrystalline infrared fibers (PIR): 3000 nm to 17 µm.
- Hollow glass waveguides: 2000 nm to 18 µm.
- Indium fluoride fibers (Le Verre Fluore): 300 nm to 5500 nm.
- Zirconium fluoride fibers (Le Verre Fluore): 300 nm to 4500 nm.
"Polycrystalline AgHal PIR-Fibers & Combi-Probes from art photonics."

## S02 p.9
"CIR- & PIR-Fibers vs Hollow Waveguides for Mid IR-Photonics".
Figure: transmission of AsS CIR fibre (high transmission from about 2 to 6 µm, dropping to zero near 6.5 µm) and AgHal PIR fibre (about 4 to 18 µm); transmission of a 2 m AgHal PIR fiber (PIR 600/700) compared with a 2 m hollow waveguide (HWG-750) between 3100 and 600 cm-1. Also shown: a 19xPIR-fiber IR-image bundle, "PIR-Fiber & HWG Cables for QCL, OPO, CO- & CO2-Lasers", "Fiber Probes for FTIR-Process-Spectroscopy".

## S02 p.10
"Selection of PIR-fibers or Hollow Waveguides for FTIR-Spectroscopy". "Hollow Waveguides can be produced with different Transmission spectra, but they possess too low NA>0.1 - which results in very poor coupling efficiency with FTIR-spectrometers, while high NA>0.3 for PIR-fibers provides much better coupling efficiency of PIR-fiber based ATR-probes with FTIR-beams - much better vs Hollow Waveguides." "Very Flexible, non-brittle and non-toxic PIR-fibers are the best in coupling with FTIR-spectrometers!"
Figure: single beam and acetone spectra of an ATR probe with PIR fiber versus hollow waveguide, 2600 to 600 cm-1; the PIR probe gives a much stronger signal.

## S02 p.11
"Test of Silica Fiber Reflection Probes with FT-NIR vs DG-spectrometers". Probes shown: "12mm Reflexion Probe", "Fish-eye DRS-Probe with 2 side windows", "DRS-Probe with 9 side looking Sapphire windows". "Comparison Test of Reflex Probes with FT-NIR spectrometer Matrix-F (Bruker) and DG-NIR spectrometer NIR-Quest (from Ocean Insight)."
Figure: reflectance spectra of a dysprosium oxide reflectance standard (Spectralon) from 5000 to 10000 cm-1 measured with an FT-NIR and with a diffraction grating NIR spectrometer; both curves show the same band positions.

## S02 p.12
"IoT Concept of NIR-LED Fiber Sensor to control Moisture Level". Shown: FlexiSens sensor ("Optimal set of wavelengths to see moisture of media in contact"; "Customized calibration for high accuracy in fast data acquisition"; "IP-address to collect data from each sensor by LAN"; "Trans-Flex or diffuse reflection").
Figure: SNV-corrected NIR spectra of starch, flour and sugar (about 1200-2100 nm); reflectance spectra and PLS regression coefficients (900-1700 nm) with a few highlighted wavelength regions; a web interface showing moisture of wood ("Water drop drying on wood surface with time").

## S02 p.13
"Application: measurement of water content in petroleum". "ATR-Probes with ATR-tips from ZrO2 (& Diamond, etc.) sealed in PEEK-shaft and protected by PEEK-tubing can be used in microwave furnaces."
Figure: MIR absorbance spectra from 4000 to 2000 cm-1 of pure oil and of oil with 0.1%, 0.5%, 1.0% and 2.0% water; absorbance between about 4000 and 3000 cm-1 increases with water content. The slide shows a laboratory spectral series; it states no process installation, accuracy or detection limit.

## S02 p.14
"NIR-Reflex & MIR-ATR-spectroscopy to control polymer extrusion in-line". "Control of API-production with PIR-fiber FTIR-spectroscopy in Pharma Industry." "Spectra of API in hot polymer obtained with Diamond cone ATR probe coupled to iC10 FTIR (MTAC)."
Figure: hot melt extruder scheme (material feeder, screw, barrel, die, die adaptor, NIR, PAT console, "Feed forward control for waste gate switch", good product / waste); photo of an NIR probe at the extruder barrel and die.

## S02 p.15
"Sensing with Combi-Fiber Probe to Fuse Mid-IR + Fluorescence Spectra" (doi 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c00080). "Only 2 wavelengths!"
Figure: ATR + autofluorescence combi probe on tissue; tissue classification of liver, breast and fat using IR absorbance at 1743 cm-1 and fluorescence at 613 nm; PCA score plots; normalized MIR absorbance (1800-1500 cm-1) and fluorescence (500-750 nm) spectra.

## S02 p.16
"Combi-Fiber Systems for ATR+Fluo/ Raman spectroscopy". "Combi probe with 2 IR-fibers coupled to FTIR-spectrometer and 2 Silica fibers coupled to Fluo or Raman spectrometers to work with 680nm & 785nm laser excitation." "Combi-ATR/ Fluo&Raman Fiber System with Bruker ALPHA, NLIR & Wasatch spectrometers." Reference: Nature Sci. Rep. 14, 7430 (2024), "Multimodal fiber probe for simultaneous mid-infrared and Raman spectroscopy".
Figure: probe schematic with CIR fibers (IR) and silica fibers (Raman) under an ATR crystal for a liquid and a solid sample; Raman and MIR spectra of refined rapeseed oil between 2600 and 3100 cm-1.

## S02 p.17
"IR-Fiber coupled IC & QC-Lasers for Biomedical Diagnostics". "PIR-fiber bundle combines radiation of 7 QC-Lasers into Arthro-Probe." "IR-fiber bundle combiner for any ICL or QCL of selected wavelength." "QCL with detachable IR-fiber cable."
Figure: gas absorption bands between 4 and 10 µm (CO2, CO, NO, H2O, NO2, SO3, SO2, CH4, NH3); emission ranges of diode lasers (below about 3 µm), interband cascade lasers (about 3-6 µm) and quantum cascade lasers (about 4-14 µm).

Figure: spectral bands with typical analytes: 0.85-1.1 µm "Fat, Protein, Moisture,..."; 1.3-1.7, 1.5-2.0 and 1.7-2.2 µm "Moisture, Polymers, API, Ethanol,.."; 3.0-3.7 and 3.7-4.5 µm "Hydrocarbons, CO2,..."; 6.0-8.0 and 8.0-10.5 µm "Ethanol, Glucose, Glutamine, org. Acids, Ammonium,...".

"Innovative Fabry-Perot tunable filters from InfraTec, LED, Filters, IR-LED from NanoPlus, spectral chips from MantiSpectra, etc. enable to develop compact spectral fiber sensors of customized design with data send to iCloud as in IoT - to enable remote process control in customized applications."
